      How to apply each file
       
       
      Power BI Desktop
      View tab, Themes, Browse for themes, pick . Every visual takes the data colours, fonts, axis and gridline colours at once.
       
      PowerPoint
      Design tab, the More arrow at the bottom right of the Themes gallery, Browse for Themes, pick . To keep it: same arrow, Save Current Theme. To make it the default: right-click it in the gallery, Set as Default Theme.
       
      Excel
      Page Layout tab, Themes, Browse for Themes, pick . Charts, tables, cell styles and the colour picker switch to your colours and fonts.
       
      Word
      Design tab, Themes, Browse for Themes, pick . Headings take your heading font, charts and SmartArt take your colours.

       What is in the pack. powerbi/<name>.json is a Power BI report theme: data colours, text classes for titles, labels and callouts, the page and visual background, axis and gridline colours, good, bad and neutral for KPIs and conditional formatting, and the table accent. office/<name>.thmx is an Office theme with a 16:9 slide master: dark and light text colours, six accents, heading and body fonts, read by PowerPoint, Excel, Word and Outlook. office/theme1.xml is the same theme as a raw part, for anyone who swaps it inside a .pptx or .xlsx by hand. python/<name>.mplstyle sets the matplotlib colour cycle, background, text, grid and spines. python/plotly-<name>.json is a plotly template with the colourway and a sequential and diverging scale. web/vega-lite-<name>.json is a Vega-Lite config object. web/<name>.css holds CSS custom properties, --theme-series-1 onwards. brand.json holds your inputs: drop it back on this page next year and you edit the same theme instead of starting again.

       
       Why the order of the data colours matters. Power BI, Office, matplotlib, plotly and Vega-Lite all hand out colours in list order: the first series gets colour 1, the second gets colour 2, and so on. So colour 1 is the colour your readers see most, and colours 1 and 2 sit next to each other in almost every chart. Put your main brand colour first, put a clear contrast second, and keep two similar colours far apart in the list. Office stores six accents, so a seventh data colour reaches Power BI and Python but not PowerPoint.

       
       Why the contrast and colour-blind checks matter. About 1 man in 12 (8%) and 1 woman in 200 have a red-green colour deficiency, so in a meeting of twenty people at least one reader usually cannot tell a red bar from a green one. The tool simulates each deficiency at full strength (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes, 2009) and measures the distance between every pair of data colours with CIEDE2000: below 10 two series start to merge on a chart, below 5 most people see one colour. Greyscale shows what the office printer does to your report. For text, WCAG 2 asks for 4.5:1 against the background for body text and 3:1 for large headings and chart marks.

       
       Privacy. Everything runs in this page. A file you drop is read by your browser and never uploaded, and the downloads are built on your machine. The share link carries your colours after the # sign, which browsers do not send to any server. The one outside call: when you type a font name the page does not know, the preview asks Google Fonts for that name so it can draw it.
